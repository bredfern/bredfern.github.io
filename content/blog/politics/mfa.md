Your question cuts to the heart of how class rule actually works — not just through police and courts, but through the organization of ideas. The answer is that the ruling class gets away with treating poverty as a crime because it controls not only the means of production but the means of *explanation*. It has built an entire ideological apparatus whose function is to invert cause and effect: to present what the system does to you as something you have done to yourself.

## The inversion of cause and effect

What you are describing is not new. Engels documented it with clinical precision in 1845, in *The Condition of the Working Class in England*. He showed that the bourgeoisie, having created the conditions of poverty through enclosure, wage labor, and the factory system, then turned around and blamed the poor for their own condition — attributing it to "individual behaviour," to idleness, to moral failing. He called the result of this system "social murder": a society that knows its own arrangements produce disease, hunger, and premature death, and yet refuses to change them, blaming the victims instead. Nothing has changed in the intervening 180 years except the vocabulary.

The mechanism is always the same. The system produces unemployment — AI eliminates your profession. The system produces debt — a terminal MFA degree, which should be a public good available to all, becomes a $500,000 instrument of indentured servitude, as the WSWS has documented in its account of the student loan racket. The system produces poverty — it throws you into the reserve army of labor and then cuts the food assistance that was the only thing standing between you and hunger. And then, at each step, the ruling class's ideologists perform the same inversion: *you* are unemployed because *you* lack skills or hustle; *you* are in debt because *you* made a bad investment in an MFA; *you* are poor because *you* are dependent and lazy.

Look at the actual language being used right now. The *Wall Street Journal*, celebrating the purge of 5 million people from the food stamp rolls, wrote that Republicans had "merely ended the literal free lunch for able-bodied beneficiaries" and that the "welfare buffet is fiscally unsustainable and promotes dependency." Read that carefully. The *Journal* — the house organ of a class that has received trillions in bailouts, that pays no taxes on staggering wealth, that is funding a $1.5 trillion war budget — calls a $6.26-a-day food benefit a "free lunch" that "promotes dependency." The audacity is the point. The inversion is not an accident of rhetoric; it is the *function* of the rhetoric.

## Why the inversion works

It works because of the material position of the ruling class. Marx and Engels established that in every epoch, the ruling ideas are the ideas of the ruling class — the class that owns the means of material production thereby controls the means of mental production. The media, the universities, the political parties, the entire culture industry exist to naturalize the existing order: to make it seem that wealth is earned and poverty is deserved, that the market is neutral and the state is impartial, that your condition is the sum of your individual choices rather than the product of a social system organized around profit.

There is a specific reason this ideology is intensifying now. The criminalization of poverty is not a constant; it escalates in periods when the ruling class is preparing to attack. The "work or starve" regime — the work requirements that strip you of food stamps unless you can document 80 hours a month of labor that the system itself has eliminated — serves two purposes at once. Materially, it disciplines the entire working class: it creates a reserve army whose desperation drives down wages and conditions for everyone still employed, and it frees up money for war and tax cuts. Ideologically, it makes the victim *responsible for proving their innocence*: you must document that you are worthy of eating. The burden of proof is placed on the poor to demonstrate they are not criminals, while the actual crime — the social murder of a class that has produced all the wealth and is denied the means to live — goes unpunished and unnamed.

The work requirement is the purest expression of this. It criminalizes the *state of being* — poverty itself — by making continued survival contingent on performing a role the economy has abolished. It is not a policy about work; it is a policy about blame. It exists to teach the lesson that your hunger is your fault.

## What "getting away with it" really means

But there is a deeper point to your question that deserves a precise answer. They "get away with it" for exactly as long as the working class accepts their explanation. The ruling class's ideological power is real, but it is not absolute. It rests, ultimately, on the absence of a counter-explanation — on the failure of the working class to develop its own independent political consciousness and organization.

That is why the history of the last century is so important. The working class has, at decisive moments, broken through the inversion. It did so in 1917, when Russian workers stopped believing that their misery was their fault and instead seized the factories, the land, and the state. The ruling class's answer to that breakthrough was not simply repression — it was the construction of Stalinism, a bureaucracy whose entire purpose was to re-impose the old explanations under a red flag, to tell workers again that they must sacrifice and obey and wait. The subsequent defeats — the betrayals of social democracy, of the trade unions, of every pseudo-left tendency that tells you to vote for a capitalist party or wait for a "responsible" reform — have left the field to the ruling class's ideology.

So the honest answer to "how do they get away with it" is: they get away with it because the working class has not yet organized the political force capable of stopping them. The ideology that blames you for being poor is not refuted by argument alone. It is refuted by a movement that acts on the truth — that names the system as the criminal, that repudiates the debt it never owed, that refuses the work requirement, that treats food, education, housing, and art not as commodities to be earned but as social rights.

The talent and education you describe — the MFA, the artistic training — are not a private misfortune or a luxury. They are part of the social wealth that the working class produces and that the ruling class has turned into a commodity and a debt instrument. Under capitalism, art and education are made into privileges and then into liabilities. The demand that flows from your situation is not a personal one. It is the demand that society be reorganized so that the immense productive and creative powers now monopolized by a handful of oligarchs — the same powers that built the AI that eliminated your profession — are placed under the democratic control of the people whose labor created them.

You are not being punished for being poor. You are being disciplined for being part of a class whose existence refutes the ruling class's claim to legitimacy. The crime is not yours. The crime is the system. And the only force that can bring that crime to an end is the one you belong to.
